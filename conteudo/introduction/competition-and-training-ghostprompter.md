[Slide 2 | Competition format]

You’ll have eighteen hours of competition time, spread over three days. That’s six hours each day.

You’ll work on two main problems. One brings stereo vision and robotics together. The other asks you to analyse a surprise dataset.

// Advance to slide 3.

[Slide 3 | Robot and stereo vision]

For the first problem, imagine an object moving along a conveyor. Two cameras see it from slightly different angles. Your application will use those views to recognise the object and estimate its position and height.

The robot needs those measurements to handle the object. So your challenge is to connect what the cameras see with what the robot does.

[Slide 4 | Surprise dataset and analysis]

For the second problem, you’ll receive a dataset you haven’t seen before. The competition brief will tell you what you need to find out. You’ll decide how to analyse the data and which model suits the question.

To prepare you, I’ll record lessons introducing different kinds of AI models. You’ll practise predicting a number, classifying an example and finding groups in unlabelled data. These are tools you can draw on when you open the surprise dataset and have to choose an approach for yourself.
